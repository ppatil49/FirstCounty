## 1. Regulation DD (Truth in Savings) — Deposit advertising — 12 CFR 1030.8

**Misleading terms — 1030.8(a)**
- An ad may not be misleading or inaccurate, and may not misrepresent the deposit contract.
- **"Free" / "no cost"** (or similar) may not describe an account if **any maintenance or activity fee may be imposed** on it (for example, a monthly fee that applies unless a balance or direct-deposit condition is met). — 1030.8(a)(2)
- The word **"profit"** may not be used to describe interest. — 1030.8(a)(3)

**Stating a rate — 1030.8(b)**
- If an ad states a rate of return, it must be stated as an **"annual percentage yield"** (the abbreviation "APY" may be used if spelled out at least once).
- The **interest rate** may also be stated, but only together with the APY and **not more conspicuously** than the APY. No other rate may be stated.

**Additional disclosures when an APY is stated — 1030.8(c)**
1. **Variable rate:** that the rate may change after the account is opened.
2. **Time period:** the period the APY is offered, or a statement that the APY is accurate as of a specified date.
3. **Minimum balance** required to obtain the advertised APY.
4. **Minimum opening deposit**, if greater than the minimum balance needed to obtain the APY.
5. **Effect of fees:** that fees could reduce earnings on the account.
6. **Time accounts (CDs):** the term (time requirement), and a statement that a **penalty will or may be imposed for early withdrawal**.

**Bonuses — 1030.8(d)**
If a bonus is advertised, the ad must state (as applicable): the APY; the **time requirement** to obtain the bonus; the **minimum balance** required to obtain the bonus; the minimum opening balance if greater; and **when the bonus will be provided**.

**Media exemptions — 1030.8(e)**
Certain disclosures may be omitted in broadcast/electronic media and outdoor media. None of the deposit ads in this exercise depend on these exemptions.

## 2. Regulation Z (Truth in Lending) — Credit advertising

### Closed-end credit (auto loans, mortgages, personal loans) — 12 CFR 1026.24
- **Rates — 1026.24(c):** If an ad states a rate of finance charge, it must state it as an **"annual percentage rate"** ("APR"). If the APR may increase after consummation, the ad must say so. A simple annual rate may appear alongside the APR but **not more conspicuously**.
- **Triggering terms — 1026.24(d):** If an ad states any of the following:
  - the amount or percentage of any **down payment**
  - the **number of payments or period of repayment** (e.g., "60 months", "30-year")
  - the **amount of any payment** (e.g., "$299/month")
  - the amount of any **finance charge**

  …then the ad must also state: (i) the down payment amount or percentage; (ii) the **terms of repayment**; and (iii) the **APR**, and whether it may increase after consummation.
- **Dwelling-secured loans — 1026.24(f), (i):** Any simple annual rate must be accompanied by the APR with equal or greater prominence. Misleading uses of "fixed" and similar terms are prohibited.

### Open-end credit (credit cards, HELOCs) — 12 CFR 1026.16
- **Home-equity plans — 1026.16(d):** Triggering terms require additional disclosures. If an ad states a **promotional or introductory rate**, it must use the word "introductory" or "intro" in immediate proximity to the rate, state how long the rate applies, and state the APR that will apply after the introductory period.
- **Promotional rates, other open-end credit (e.g., credit cards) — 1026.16(g):** Same core requirement: label the rate **"introductory"/"intro"**, state the **time period**, and state the **APR that will apply after** the promotional period.

## 3. FDIC — Official advertising statement and insured status — 12 CFR Part 328

- **Official advertising statement — Subpart A:** Ads promoting **deposit products** must include **"Member FDIC"** or "Member of the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation" (or the FDIC symbol/logo). Ads that do **not** promote deposit products (for example, loan-only ads) are not required to carry it. Newer rules extend FDIC signage requirements to digital channels (websites, mobile apps) and require distinguishing insured deposits from non-deposit products.
- **Misrepresentation — Subpart B:** A bank may not represent or imply that a **non-deposit product** (investments, annuities, crypto, insurance) is FDIC-insured, and may not use the FDIC name or logo in a way that suggests this.

## 4. Non-deposit investment products — disclosures

Interagency Statement on Retail Sales of Nondeposit Investment Products; FINRA Rule 2210.
- Ads for investments or annuities sold through the bank or an affiliate must clearly state that the products are **Not FDIC Insured – Not Bank Guaranteed – May Lose Value** (and, where applicable, Not a Deposit and Not Insured by Any Federal Government Agency).
- Ads must not describe investment returns as "safe" or "guaranteed" in a way that implies bank or FDIC backing.

## 5. Fair lending and fair housing

- **Equal Housing Lender — 12 CFR 338.3 (FDIC-supervised banks):** Ads for **residential real estate–related loans** (mortgages, home equity, HELOCs) must include the Equal Housing Lender logo or legend. For audio-only ads, the words "Equal Housing Lender" are used.
- **Discouragement — Regulation B, 12 CFR 1002.4(b); Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604(c):** Ads may not use words, images or targeting that would discourage applicants on a prohibited basis (e.g., age, race, national origin, sex, marital status, familial status, receipt of public assistance).

## 6. UDAAP — Unfair, deceptive or abusive acts or practices

12 U.S.C. 5531 and 5536 (Dodd-Frank); Section 5 of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. 45).
- Claims must be truthful and **substantiated**. Superlatives ("lowest rates in Connecticut", "guaranteed best") need support.
- Fine print may not contradict the headline ("no strings attached" next to fee conditions).
- The net impression of the ad is what matters, not just the literal words.

---

## 7. FCRA — Prescreened offers of credit

15 U.S.C. 1681m(d); Regulation V, 12 CFR 1022.54.
- Written **prescreened** credit or insurance offers must include the **prescreen opt-out notice**, in both a **short form** (on the first page) and a **long form**, telling consumers how to opt out of prescreened offers (1-888-5-OPT-OUT / www.optoutprescreen.com).
- Offers must be "firm offers of credit". Conditions must be disclosed.

## 8. CAN-SPAM — Commercial email

15 U.S.C. 7704; 16 CFR Part 316.
- Commercial email must include: a working **unsubscribe/opt-out mechanism**; the sender's **valid physical postal address**; identification as an advertisement (where applicable); and **non-deceptive** header and subject lines.
